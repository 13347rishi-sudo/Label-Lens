About LabelLens

LabelLens is an offline, edge-native food packaging scanner and computational nutrition intelligence platform. Developed as an advanced school science exhibition project, LabelLens tackles one of modern society's most pervasive public health crises: the widespread consumption of ultra-processed food masked behind deceptive, microscopic front-of-package marketing claims.

Our Core Mission

Modern food manufacturers routinely employ sophisticated formulation tactics to obscure poor nutritional profiles. They split sugars into exotic aliases like maltodextrin, invert syrup, and date concentrate to push "Sugar" down the ingredients list. They substitute heart-healthy fats with cheap industrial palm olein and saturate products with artificial additives. LabelLens democratizes food transparency by empowering consumers, students, and families with instant, uncompromising clinical intelligence directly in their browser or at exhibition kiosks.

Scientific & Technological Pillars

  • European Nutri-Score 2.0 Algorithm: Full computational implementation of the official 2023/2024 revision developed by independent European nutritional scientists. Calculates bad points (N) and good points (P) with strict protein capping to prevent junk foods from gaming health ratings.
  • Multimodal Edge Vision AI: Powered by local vision-language models (Ollama with qwen3-vl:8b and qwen3.5:2b) capable of reading fine print, barcodes, and ingredients panels with 0ms cloud latency and complete offline privacy.
  • Ingredient Knowledge Graph: An exhaustive relational dataset tracking over 50 covert sweetener aliases, 20 industrial palm oil derivatives, 14 EU statutory allergens, and European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) food additive concern classifications.
  • Personalized Clinical Health Lenses: Instant contextual evaluation tailored for diabetics, children under 10, hypertensive individuals, and severe nut allergy sufferers.
  • Whole-Food Swapping Engine: Suggests tangible, evidence-backed alternatives, emphasizing traditional unprocessed whole foods like millets, pulses, foxnuts (makhana), and tender coconut water.

Open Science Commitment

LabelLens is completely free, open-source, and non-commercial. It operates without user tracking, paywalls, or remote cloud surveillance. All datasets and algorithms are open for public education and school science demonstration.
